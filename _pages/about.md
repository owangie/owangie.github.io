I didn't start in data. I started in languages: Portuguese and Estonian, alongside foreign affairs, in Beijing. At 19 I added a third, Python, with *Python Crash Course*, my first programming book. Programming is a language too, and it's the one I ended up speaking every day (sorry, Estonian). While still a student, I was hired as an assistant representative in China's foreign aid programme at the Ministry of Commerce (think JICA or KOICA), coordinating three month training seminars for senior officials from the least developed countries. Then I spent a summer at Amazon Web Services in Hong Kong, where I still remember the engineer on the team who was fine-tuning Alexa. The numbers got serious during a Master of Public Policy in development economics at the University of Maryland, where I did a capstone with the World Resources Institute in Washington, DC, and worked with the UN Foundation.

Then came four years at UNDP in New York as a data scientist. I built the Power BI dashboards country offices used to follow programme results, and wrote code that sorted and summarised more than 15,000 evaluation reports. That was my first real text mining: the evidence was all there, locked inside documents. Along the way I also worked at the World Food Programme and the UN Capital Development Fund.

Now I'm an evaluation associate at the Green Climate Fund's Independent Evaluation Unit in Incheon, Korea. I build datasets from project portfolios, write Python that gets language models to read project documents, and turn the results into charts for evaluation reports. In my first months I started using machine learning to mine evaluation evidence, and I set up the unit's GitHub, which at first nobody knew what to do with. I was elected AI champion for the organisation, and I'm currently doing a Microsoft training programme on finding business use cases for agentic AI.

Outside work I help an auction house part time, and I have a soft spot for Chinese antiques. The job there is the same as mine at work: every object has a story, and you build it from evidence, a mark here, a record there, until the history holds up. So if I need a title for what I do, data curator is the closest one.
