Onki Wong (Angie) is an evaluation associate at the Independent Evaluation Unit of the Green Climate Fund, working on portfolio analysis and AI for evaluation. She builds the datasets, econometric analyses and language model pipelines behind the unit's independent evaluations.

She was the sole quantitative analyst for the evaluation of the Fund's approach to country ownership (2025) and wrote its quantitative annex. Her analysis of language and approval times was cited by the Fund's Secretariat in its Board paper on multilingualism ([GCF/B.43/Inf.12](https://www.greenclimate.fund/board-document/gcf-b43-inf12)). She built the language model pipeline that screened the funding proposal portfolio for the evaluation of the Fund's private sector approach, and, as lead data analyst for the synthesis of the Readiness Programme, a retrieval system that drew on 34 earlier evaluations and policy documents, about 1,900 pages. She also set up the unit's GitHub, and the organisation elected her its AI champion.

Before joining the Fund in 2024, she spent four years as a data scientist at UNDP in New York. She developed more than 30 Power BI dashboards used across country offices, including an Evaluation Scorecard tracking five evaluation indicators, and used topic modelling to map more than 15,000 evaluation reports to the Sustainable Development Goals. On assignment to the UN Office for South-South Cooperation, she built its first trust fund dashboard and trained six staff to run it. Earlier she worked at the World Food Programme, the UN Capital Development Fund, Amazon Web Services and China's Ministry of Commerce.

She holds a Master of Public Policy from the University of Maryland, where she studied impact evaluation with Professor Susan W. Parker and econometrics with Professor Alok Bhargava, and wrote her thesis with the World Resources Institute under Professor Anand Patwardhan. She holds a B.A. in Portuguese and Foreign Affairs from Beijing Foreign Studies University.

Outside work, she is fond of history and art and has spent time helping at an auction house. Tracing an antique back to its age and maker is not so different from evaluating a project: you read the marks and the records, and tell only the story the evidence supports.
